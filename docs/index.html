How it works

Learn a model of the engines, then optimize inside it

1 · PROBE

Ask real engines buyer questions, many times. Record pages read and brands named.

2 · SIMULATE

A cheap model answers from the same pages; validated against the real engines.

3 · EDIT

A Thompson-sampling bandit picks one of 7 edit types; an LLM applies it.

4 · CHECK

A claim checker rejects anything not supported by the original page.

5 · PROVE

Pre-registered head-to-head on the real engine, held-out questions.

Step 1 · What the engines recommend today

Two incumbents take most answers; engines disagree on the challengers

Share of answers naming the brand in its top 3 · 40 cobot buyer questions · Claude Opus 5.5, OpenAI gpt-5.5, Perplexity

Step 2 · Is the simulator any good?

Yes for overall rankings. No for single questions — and we say so.

Our first simulator (told to use "only these results") scored r = −0.05: it trusted vendors' own "best cobot" blogs. Letting it blend sources with its own knowledge — as real engines do — fixed it.

Step 5 · The pre-registered result FAIL

The real engine didn't confirm the gains — and showed us why

Noise: the same unchanged page, asked twice, scored 50% vs 38% (Franka) and 25% vs 38% (Elite).

So: simulated gains measured with 16 runs per edit sat inside that noise; the optimizer kept lucky edits.

Fix: many more simulator runs per edit, more questions, more final trials.
